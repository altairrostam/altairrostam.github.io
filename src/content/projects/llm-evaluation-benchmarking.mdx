## Overview

The evaluation harness made model comparisons repeatable by separating datasets, prompts, expected behavior, scoring rubrics, and result analysis.

## Problem

Model changes are easy to feel and hard to measure. Without a repeatable harness, teams rely on a handful of anecdotal prompts and miss regressions.

## Architecture

The system organized evaluation cases into suites, ran models against consistent prompts, captured structured outputs, and compared results across scoring dimensions.

## Tradeoffs

Strict rubrics improved comparability but could underrepresent subjective quality. Free-form review caught more nuance but made regressions harder to track.

## Technical Decisions

The harness preserved raw model outputs alongside normalized scores so reviewers could inspect why a result passed or failed.

## Challenges

The most difficult evaluation cases were those where correctness depended on context, tone, or operational judgment rather than a single factual answer.

## Lessons Learned

Evaluation is a product surface for the builders of the system. If the harness is hard to update, it will quietly stop reflecting reality.
